Concept 1 of 25 · Part A: Where is the liquidity?

Swing points (STH / STL)

A swing high is a peak, a swing low is a trough. ICT's whole model starts here, because stop-loss orders cluster just beyond them.

coded · learn command availableModule M1

The idea

Every trader who is long puts a stop below the most recent low. Every short seller puts a stop above the most recent high. So the market's turning points are also its order clusters. ICT calls those clusters liquidity. Before you can find liquidity, you need an exact, repeatable definition of a turning point.

Exact rule in the engine
STH at bar i:  high[i] > high[i-1]  AND  high[i] > high[i+1]
STL at bar i:  low[i]  < low[i-1]   AND  low[i]  < low[i+1]
Confirmed at:  close of bar i+1   (config: fractal_left / fractal_right / strict)

How to spot it

  1. Look at three candles side by side.
  2. If the middle one has the highest high, it is a short-term high. If it has the lowest low, it is a short-term low. One candle can be both.
  3. Do not mark it until the third candle has closed. Until then the middle bar is only a candidate; the next bar could still make a higher high.

Why it matters in a trade

  • Stops go beyond a swing point.
  • Targets are the next swing on the other side.
  • Structure: higher highs and higher lows mean up, lower highs and lower lows mean down, and a break of the last swing is a market structure shift.
Common mistakes
  • Marking a swing on the day it forms. That is lookahead: in real time you do not know yet.
  • Using 5-bar or 'eyeballed' swings and then wondering why your backtest disagrees with your chart.
  • Treating every short-term swing as important. Most are noise; the next page shows how to rank them.
Exercise

Open NIFTY 15-minute on TradingView. Mark every STH and STL by hand on the last 60 bars, then run python -m scripts.learn swings --bars 60 and compare. Every difference teaches you something about the rule.
