Concept 3 of 25 · Part A: Where is the liquidity?

Open float

A swing level that has never been revisited still has orders resting beyond it. Once price trades through, those orders are filled and the level is spent.

coded · learn command availableModule M1

The idea

Think of every unbroken swing as an unpaid bill. Stops above an old high are buy orders waiting to be triggered. Price 'pays the bill' by trading through it. ICT's Open Float lessons focus on the levels still unpaid, because those are where the market is likely to go next.

Exact rule in the engine
A swing high is BROKEN on the first later bar whose high > swing price
(or whose close > swing price, if config break_on: close).
Open float = all swings not yet broken.
Open highs above price = BUY-SIDE liquidity. Open lows below = SELL-SIDE liquidity.

Why it matters

  • The nearest strong open level above and below price frames the draw on liquidity: where price is likely heading.
  • An open level is a natural target. A spent level is not.
  • Higher-tier open levels (ITH/LTH) carry more orders than short-term ones.
Common mistakes
  • Using a level that was already taken as a target.
  • Ignoring old weekly levels far away. They stay open for months and act like magnets.
Exercise

On NIFTY weekly, list 5 open highs above price and 5 open lows below. Check them against the dotted lines in reports/NIFTY_weekly_swings.png after running python -m scripts.run_swings NIFTY.
