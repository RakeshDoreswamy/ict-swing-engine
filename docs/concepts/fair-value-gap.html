Concept 7 of 25 · Part B: Where will price react?

Fair value gap (FVG) and inverse FVG

When candle 2 moves so fast that candles 1 and 3 do not overlap, the empty space is a fair value gap: one-sided trading that price often comes back to rebalance.

explained · code comingModule M3

The idea

In the gap, only buyers (bullish FVG) or only sellers (bearish FVG) were active. ICT teaches that the market's algorithm returns to 'offer fair value' on the other side. In practice, an FVG is a high-probability entry zone on a pullback.

Exact rule in the engine
Bullish FVG (middle bar i): low[i+1] > high[i-1]   zone = [high[i-1], low[i+1]]
Bearish FVG (middle bar i): high[i+1] < low[i-1]   zone = [high[i+1], low[i-1]]
CE (consequent encroachment) = 50% of the zone
Filled: a later bar trades through the far edge
Inverse FVG: a later bar CLOSES through the far edge; the zone now works from the other side
Minimum size: fvg_min_size_atr × ATR

How to trade it

  1. Only use FVGs created by displacement in the direction of your bias.
  2. Prefer an FVG inside discount (for longs) and near an HTF zone.
  3. Place a limit at the CE (50%). The stop goes beyond the swing that started the move, not just beyond the gap.
  4. If price closes through the whole gap, it has failed. Treat it as an inverse FVG, and stop looking for longs there.
Common mistakes
  • Trading every FVG. Most small ones on 15m are noise; filter by size and context.
  • Putting the stop just under the FVG. It gets taken by normal pullbacks.
Exercise

On NIFTY daily, mark the last 5 FVGs. For each, record: was it filled, did the CE hold, and did it become an inverse FVG?
