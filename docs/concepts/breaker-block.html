Concept 9 of 25 · Part B: Where will price react?

Breaker block

When an order block fails after a liquidity sweep, the same zone tends to act from the other side: old support becomes resistance.

explained · code comingModule M3

The idea

A breaker tells a story in three acts. First, a high is swept (liquidity taken). Then price displaces down, through the bullish order block that launched the last leg up. Those trapped longs now want out at breakeven, so when price comes back up into the zone, their selling plus the new shorts make it resistance.

Exact rule in the engine
Bearish breaker: a bullish OB where a later bar CLOSES below its low (ideally after a buy-side sweep)
Bullish breaker: a bearish OB where a later bar CLOSES above its high (ideally after a sell-side sweep)
Entry: retest of the zone from the new side
Common mistakes
  • Calling any broken OB a breaker. The strongest ones follow a sweep and a displacement through the zone.
  • Entering on the break itself instead of the retest.
Exercise

On NIFTY daily, find one bullish OB that later failed. Did price retest it from below, and did it hold as resistance?
